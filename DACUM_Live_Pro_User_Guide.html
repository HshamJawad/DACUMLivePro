🗺 Overview

What is DACUM Live Pro?

DACUM Live Pro is a browser-based occupational analysis platform that walks curriculum developers and TVET specialists through the whole chain — from duties and tasks to task analysis, competencies, the occupational standard, learning outcomes, training modules and the module curriculum — with AI assistance at every stage, live workshop voting, and a full interface in English, French and Arabic.

✨
Full Draft generatorOne run builds the whole chain — duties, tasks, supporting information, clusters, criteria, outcomes and modules.
🤖
AI in every tabGeneration cards sit in Duties & Tasks, Additional Info, Task Analysis, Competency Clusters, Learning Outcomes and Module Mapping.
📜
Occupational StandardThe Occupational Profile & Standard assembled from your chart and competencies — with a list of what is still missing.
📘
Module curriculumA curriculum document per module — time table, teaching plan per outcome and resources — exported to Word.
🌐
Three languages, full RTLEnglish, French and Arabic across the interface, the exports and this guide.
📡
Live workshop votingShare a link or QR code; participants rate tasks from any browser and results aggregate instantly.
📁
Multi-project sidebarRun several occupational analyses side by side and switch between them with full state preserved.
📄
PDF & Word exportCharts, verification reports, the Occupational Standard and module curricula — with correctly shaped Arabic.
📴
Works offlineInstallable as an app. Every tab works without a connection — only AI generation and live voting need the network.
💾
JSON save / importSave projects as portable JSON files. Import one or many — each becomes a project in the sidebar.
ℹ️
DACUM Live Pro runs entirely in the browser — nothing to install. Your data lives in the browser’s storage and in the JSON files you download; it is never uploaded anywhere. Two features do reach the network: AI generation and live workshop voting.
🔗
DACUM Lite and DACUM Live Pro. DACUM Lite is the simpler companion tool for building the chart itself: Chart Info, Duties & Tasks and Additional Info. A chart started in Lite opens here with Import, and the work carries on with Task Verification and the tabs that follow.
🚀 Quick Start

Quick Start: how to use DACUM Live Pro

Eleven steps from an empty project to a curriculum. Only the first two are required; follow the links for the details of each step.

1

Chart Info: enter the occupation title, job title and scope of work.

2

Duties & Tasks: generate them with AI, or add duties and tasks by hand.

3

Additional Info: add knowledge, skills, behaviours, tools and the Skills Level Matrix.

4

Task Verification: rate the tasks through workshop voting or individual ratings (optional).

5

Task Selection: choose the tasks to train and analyse (optional).

6

Task Analysis: break each task down into steps, knowledge, skills, tools, safety and criteria (optional).

7

Competency Clusters: group related tasks into competencies and define their range and performance criteria.

8

Occupational Standard: check what is still missing and export the standard (Word).

9

Learning Outcomes: turn performance criteria into learning outcomes.

10

Module Mapping: organise the outcomes into modules with levels and codes.

11

Module Curriculum CUR/CBC: write each module’s curriculum and export it (Word).

12

Export: the chart as PDF or Word, and the project as a JSON file.

✨
In a hurry? ✨ Generate Full Draft on the Chart Info tab runs steps 2, 3, 6, 8 and 9 in one go — then review each tab with your panel.

The sidebar lets you create, switch, rename and delete projects without losing work.

📁

Project cardsEach card shows the name, date, duty count and task count — and its content languages when set

1

2

3

4

5

6

🔄

Auto-saveYour work is never lost

Every edit triggers a debounced auto-save to local storage. Switching projects saves the current one first, and if the browser closes with unsaved edits a recovery prompt appears the next time you open the tool.

💾

Where projects are storedOrdinary storage, or a larger one when needed

Projects are kept in the browser. When they outgrow its ordinary storage (about 3.5 MB, which images and long charts reach quickly), the tool moves them automatically to a larger store (IndexedDB): it copies them, reads the copy back to check it, and only then removes the old one.

The 💾 Storage line under the project list shows where the projects are and how much space they use. Click it to move them by hand.

🌐

Content languages and translationOne project, its original and its translations

The language switcher in the top bar changes the screens only. ⚙️ Settings → 🌐 Languages holds the language of the project’s content: set its original language, then translate it with AI (From → To). The translation is stored with the project, so switching between the original and its translations later is instant and makes no AI call. Only texts that have no translation yet are sent — after you change a task, only that task is translated again.

The version that is shown is what every tab, every export (Word, PDF, Standard, Curriculum) and every AI card uses. Edits made while a translation is shown go into that translation; texts added there are kept and can be translated back into the original. Review a translation side by side with the original and mark it Reviewed. IDs, ratings, priorities and links between duties, tasks, criteria and outcomes are never changed by a translation. Modules sent to Module Builder carry the translations with them.

⚠️
Browser storage is not a backup. Clearing the browser’s site data deletes the projects with it — export the JSON file regularly.
💡
Collapse the sidebar with the ◀ toggle to gain screen space while editing cards or presenting during a workshop.

Running a full draftSix stages, one run

1

Fill in Occupation Title, Job Title and Scope of Work on the Chart Info tab. All three are required here — every stage inherits the boundary they set, so a gap in them compounds through six stages instead of one.

2

Click ✨ Generate Full Draft to open the generator window.

3

Choose how far down the chain to go — you can stop at duties and tasks, or continue all the way to training modules.

4

Tick the optional extras — Supporting Information is not required by any later stage, so it is safe to skip.

5

Read the notices about what the run will replace — they name the work already tied to the current content — then click Start Generating.

6

Stages complete one after another. You can press Stop at any point — everything already generated is kept. If a stage fails, everything before it is saved and you can retry from that stage.

7

When it finishes, open each tab and review. Every tab carries a 🔁 Regenerate from here button for when you change something upstream.

📸
On a project that already has content, the generator lists what will be replaced and asks you to confirm, then saves a snapshot first — restore it from the 🔍 lens if you change your mind. A stage turns green only when its content really changed; if the AI service is unavailable, the run stops and says so.
⚖️
Task Verification is not generated. Importance, frequency and difficulty are the judgement of a panel of practitioners; their value comes from who produced them. Generated numbers would look identical on the chart and carry none of that authority — so the generator leaves that stage to your panel.
🛡

Draft ratings for preparationOptional — and never silently promoted

If you want provisional ratings to prepare the workshop, the generator can produce them as an explicit option. They arrive tagged unverified: a banner sits above them, they are excluded from PDF and Word exports and from the verification report, and they are never counted as panel data.

Run the workshop, correct them with the panel, then click Accept as verified. Only then do they enter exports — and after that nothing distinguishes them from collected data, which is exactly why the tool asks you to confirm.

🔒
The interface language is locked while a full draft is running. Switching mid-run would leave the early stages in one language and the rest in another.

🧹

Refine AI ResultsAppears after a generation

After duties and tasks are generated, a Refine Results card appears under the AI card. It removes duplicates, normalises the wording, and cuts result clauses and fragments from the generated tasks. It works on duties and tasks only.

📝
Task writing format: action verb + object + context (when it matters).
Calibrate testing equipment according to manufacturer specifications.
Use concrete occupational verbs — install, inspect, calibrate, diagnose — and avoid cognitive ones such as learn, understand or know.
🔗
Regenerating never reuses the ids of earlier tasks, so the ratings, Task Analysis and cluster places of old tasks cannot attach themselves to new ones. Before anything is replaced, the confirmation lists the work tied to the current tasks.

☑️ Task Selection

Task Selection tabNew

The SCID step between verification and analysis: decide which tasks will be trained and analysed in detail. It is optional — every task stays selected until you change it, so skipping the tab changes nothing.

☑️

Selecting the tasks

1

Open Task Selection in the sidebar — after Task Verification. The count at the top shows how many tasks are selected.

2

Pick a rule — Importance ≥ x and Difficulty ≥ y (2 and 2 by default) or the top N tasks by Priority Index — and click Suggest selection. Tasks not yet rated are left as they are; Not rated yet takes you to their row in Task Verification.

3

Adjust the ticks by hand. A task left out can carry a reason — low priority, low importance, learned on the job, rarely performed, already mastered, or other.

4

Optional: tick “Is the task performed?” and enter the share of workers who perform each task. Tasks below the threshold (25% by default) are set aside; tasks marked 🆕 New are kept whatever the figure.

5

Changed your mind? ↶ Undo / ↪ Redo in the toolbar step through the selection while this tab is open.

🔗
What follows the selection. Task Analysis lists the selected tasks (Show unselected brings the others back, greyed). In Competency Clusters the tasks left out sit in a folded group, AI grouping uses selected tasks only, and Move them out of the competencies takes left-out tasks out after a confirmation. The Word and PDF exports add a Selected for training column and analyse the selected tasks; Module Builder receives the selection. The DACUM chart, the Occupational Standard and the verification results still list every task — they describe the occupation.

Verified DACUM chartRead-only, rebuilt each time it opens

Open 📋 Verified DACUM chart from Task Analysis or Duties & Tasks. Each task card shows its priority band (high: top 30% by Priority Index, medium: the next 40%, low: the rest — dashed when not rated), its rank, whether it is selected for training (or greyed with the reason) and its Task Analysis status (✓ ◐ ○). Filter All tasks / Selected for training only, and print it. Until every task is rated and every selected task analysed, the button is dimmed and says what is missing — you can still open the chart. Edit the tasks and Edit the selection lead to where those are changed.

🔬 Task Analysis

Task Analysis tabNew

For each task, break down what the worker must know, do, use, watch for and be judged against — the bridge between Task Verification and Competency Clusters. The tab is optional: you can skip it and come back at any time. Once a selection exists, the list shows the tasks selected for training.

Section What to write
Performance stepsThe steps in the order they are performed, one per line.
Required knowledgeWhat the worker must understand to perform this task.
Required skillsWhat the worker must be able to do.
Worker behavioursThe behaviours that matter for this task — picked from the Additional Info list or drafted with AI.
Performance criteriaHow competent performance of the task is judged, one criterion per line.
Performance standardThe reference performance is measured against, e.g. manufacturer specifications.
Tools, equipment & materialsWhat this task specifically uses.
Safety / OSHHazards, precautions and PPE tied to the task.
Decisions / critical pointsJudgements the worker must make, and points where things go wrong.
Conditions / work environmentWhere and how the task is normally performed — a short paragraph.
Common errors / troubleshootingTypical mistakes and how to recognise or correct them.
🔬

Working through the tasks

1

Pick a task from the list. A dot shows its status: not started, in progress or completed.

2

Fill the sections by hand. List sections take one item per line, and items can be reordered.

3

For knowledge, skills, worker behaviours and tools, pick from Additional Info copies lines you already wrote there. The copies are independent: editing them never changes the source.

4

Or click ✨ Generate with AI and tick the sections you want. Sections that already have content start unticked and are replaced only if you tick them.

5

Need a section the list does not have — Quality Requirements, for example? Click ➕ Add section under the last card. It is added to every task of the project; rename it with ✏️ or remove it with ✕ Delete section. Added sections go into the Word and PDF exports and to Module Builder.

🎓
Criteria and outcomesTask Analysis criteria appear in the Learning Outcomes source list, and the clusters’ AI writes competency criteria that complement them.
📘
Module curriculumSuggest from Task Analysis fills a module’s tools, equipment, PPE and materials.
🧱
Module BuilderSteps become activity sheets, knowledge becomes information sheets, criteria become assessment criteria.
📄
ExportsAnalysed tasks are printed as an appendix of the PDF and Word chart.
⚠️
Generated sections are a draft built from your chart. Review them with occupational experts — safety above all. If you replace performance criteria that learning outcomes already use, the dialog warns you, and the outcomes keep their links, flagged ⚠ for review.
🧩 Clusters

Competency Clusters tab

Group related tasks into competency clusters, then define the range of variables and the performance criteria for each.

1

From Task Verification or Task Analysis, click ⚡ Proceed to competency clustering. You may also proceed without verifying.

2

Tasks appear in the Available Tasks pool, highest priority index first.

3

Select tasks and click Create cluster, or let the AI assistant suggest groupings across duties by shared purpose, process or skill.

4

Write the performance criteria — what competent performance looks like, in observable and measurable terms.

5

Add the range of variables — the conditions, contexts and materials across which the competency must hold.

🧩

Editing the tasks of a competency

Inside each competency you can move tasks up or down, delete them, or ＋ Add Task. A task added here is marked Added during Competency Clustering: it belongs to the competency, not to the Occupational Profile.

Clusters stay in step with Duties & Tasks: new tasks join Available Tasks, reworded tasks are updated, and tasks deleted from the chart are flagged ⚠ for your review. A notice lists what changed.

🎯

Competency criteria and their tasks

A competency criterion describes integrated performance across the tasks of the competency, so it traces to all of them. A criterion written in Task Analysis belongs to one task, so its tracing is exact — write those early, as a draft.

In each competency the Task Analysis criteria appear first, under 🔬 From Task Analysis (read-only), and the criteria you type in the box follow them. Adding Task Analysis criteria later renumbers the typed ones — a notice says so, and learning outcomes follow automatically. A typed criterion that repeats a Task Analysis one is flagged ⚠, because it would be counted twice.

🧩
Also on this tab. A very significant task — highly important and difficult — may be a competency by itself; 4–12 tasks remains the usual size. ↶ Undo / ↪ Redo cover every step here, AI steps included. Soft 💬 wording notes flag a competency name or a criterion worth a second look; they never block or change anything.
💡
Performance criteria are the bridge between the chart and the curriculum. Each one should be specific, observable and measurable — they become the raw material for the Learning Outcomes tab, and a vague criterion produces a vague outcome.
📜 Occupational Standard

Occupational Standard tabNew

A live view of the Occupational Profile & Standard document, assembled from what you have already entered. Nothing is typed here: every section links back to the tab where it is edited.

Part Contents
Part 1 — Occupational ProfileThe panel, the duties and tasks with their codes, and the supporting information: behaviours, knowledge, skills, trends, career path and acronyms.
Part 2 — Occupational StandardThe identification block, the employability matrix, each competency with its range, related tasks and numbered performance criteria (5.1, 5.2 …), and the tools.
1

Open Occupational Standard in the sidebar — right after Competency Clusters.

2

The completeness pill at the top shows how much of the document is filled. Click it to list what is still missing; each item has an Edit in … link to the tab that fixes it.

3

Click Export Occupational Standard (Word) — the same file as Standard → Occupational Standard in the toolbar.

🏛
The reference code, endorsement, approval and review dates print as labelled blank lines: they are “filled in institutionally after export”, by the body that approves the standard.
🔢
Performance criteria are numbered n.1, n.2 in this document, so a module descriptor that cites “5.6” points at something a reader can find.
🎓 Outcomes

Learning Outcomes tab

Turn performance criteria into learning outcomes — the point where occupational analysis becomes training design.

🎓

From criteria to outcomes

1

The Performance Criteria (Source) list holds every criterion — from the clusters and from Task Analysis — under sticky competency headings. Hide used criteria shows only those not yet in an outcome.

2

Tick criteria. A bar at the bottom of the screen offers Create LO or Create 1 LO from n criteria.

3

Type the statement straight into each outcome card — it saves automatically. Enter moves to the next outcome, Use criterion text fills an empty one, and Next empty jumps to the next outcome without a statement.

4

Or use the AI card: choose a pattern and generate from the ticked criteria — or, with none ticked, from every unused one.

Pattern How it maps When to use it
A — One-to-oneOne learning outcome per performance criterion.Fine-grained assessment; strong traceability from criterion to outcome.
B — Many-to-oneSeveral related criteria combine into one broader outcome.Shorter outcome lists; suits modular or credit-based structures.
C — HybridMixes both, choosing per cluster.Real curricula rarely fit one shape throughout.
➕
Added, never replacedGenerated outcomes join the list; anything you wrote by hand stays exactly as it was.
🔗
Traceability preservedEach outcome records which criteria it addresses, so the line back to the original task never breaks.
🔢
Numbering follows positionLO1, LO2 … renumber after a deletion or a change in the clusters.
⚠️
Changes at the sourceWhen a criterion is reworded or deleted in the clusters or in Task Analysis, its link is flagged ⚠ — review it, then keep or remove it.
↩
Undo on this tabUndo / Redo in the toolbar reverse deletions and unlinks here; a toast also offers Undo for seven seconds.
❓
Design guidelinesThe ? beside Performance Criteria (Source) explains how to group criteria into outcomes and write the statement.
💬
Wording notesA 💬 note suggests a second look — an outcome that does not start with an action verb, states a purpose or runs long. Suggestions only: keep the text if it is right.
📦 Modules

Module Mapping tab

Organise learning outcomes into training modules — the last step before curriculum writing begins.

📦

Building modulesBy hand, or in one click

1

Select outcomes from the available list and click ✨ Create new module, then name it.

2

Or use ⚡ One module per outcome — instant, offline, and a reasonable starting skeleton.

3

Or use 🤖 AI group & sequence — it groups related outcomes, names and orders the modules, and shows its reasoning under each title. Options: Keep existing modules and Suggest a level for each module.

4

On each module card set the Level, Track / code prefix, Code and Short name. The code is automatic — track + level + position, e.g. CMT 1-1 — until you type your own; ↺ returns to the automatic code.

5

Build in Module Builder on a module sends it — with its outcomes, criteria, source tasks and Task Analysis — to Module Builder.

🪜
Number of levelsSet how many levels the programme has. The AI card, the coverage matrix and the level fields all use the same setting.
🏷
Show modules asCode, number (M1) or both — followed by the cards, the lists, the Module Curriculum tab and the exports.
🗺
Coverage matrixEvery performance criterion and the module and level where it is taught. Show gaps only lists what is not taught yet.
❓
Design guidelinesThe ? beside Modules explains how to build modules and assign levels: prerequisites, complexity and autonomy, specialisation, spiral progression.
🏛️
Qualifications frameworkTick Use a qualifications framework (TVQF/NQF) to give each module its framework level and descriptor. It is separate from the programme level, prints in the programme structure of the exports and goes to Module Builder.
🧭
Traceability Map🧭 Traceability Map (here and in Competency Clusters) draws the whole chain: duty → task → competency → criterion → learning outcome → module. Click a box to light up its chain, then Show only this chain; filter by duty, competency or module; hide columns; Show gaps marks what is not linked yet. Read-only. On a phone it becomes a list of tasks, each opening onto its chain.
🔁
AI grouping replaces the existing modules unless Keep existing modules is ticked; your learning outcomes are never touched. If you regenerate outcomes upstream, use 🔁 Regenerate from here on this tab rather than rebuilding by hand.
📘 Module Curriculum

Module Curriculum tab (CUR/CBC)New

The last step of the chain: a curriculum document for each module, built on its learning outcomes and their criteria. Modules must exist in Module Mapping first.

📘

Writing a module curriculum

1

Choose the module at the top; ◀ ▶ move between modules. The percentage shows how complete it is.

2

Programme settings — programme name and learners per group — are saved once for the whole project.

3

1 · Module header. Title, level, track and code come from Module Mapping. Add the purpose statement, the pre-requisite modules and the credits.

4

The Time table computes the hours: credits × hours per credit, split into theory, practical, formative assessment, industry practice and summative assessment by percentages — shared by all modules or for this module only. The parts always add up.

5

2 · Learning outcomes. For each outcome: context, methodology, discussion, demonstration, practice, self-directed learning, formative assessment statements (Suggest from criteria) and methods. Hours are shared automatically in proportion to the criteria; type a value to set your own.

6

3 · Module resources. Tools, equipment, PPE and materials (Suggest from Task Analysis), recommended resources and facilities.

7

Click Export this module (Word), or use Standard → Module Curriculum — CUR in the toolbar.

💡
💡 Suggest credits for all modules (next to the credits field) shares the programme’s total credits between the modules — by training load (Priority Index × difficulty of their tasks) or by the number of criteria. A preview comes first; nothing changes until you apply it. Typing credits by hand still works.
🗂
The file name is built from a prefix (CUR by default, or CBC), the module code, the short name and the level — e.g. CUR_CMCN 1-1 Hardware L1 En.docx. A preview shows it before you export.
❓
The ? next to each field explains what to write, with one example. In the CUR document each learning outcome starts on a new page and empty fields print as “—”. The CBC export is coming soon.
🌐 Languages

Three languages, end to endNew

English, French and Arabic run through the whole tool — interface, prompts, alerts, generated content and exported documents.

🔄
Switch anywhereUse the language control in the toolbar. Your choice is remembered the next time you open the tool.
↔️
Right-to-left layoutArabic mirrors the entire interface — sidebar, cards, tables and toolbars — and uses the Cairo typeface throughout.
🤖
Generation follows the languageContent is generated in the language of the content shown. Without content languages that is the interface language, so an Arabic session produces an Arabic chart; with them, set it in Settings → Languages.
📄
Exports follow it tooPDF and Word carry the language and reading direction of the content shown — so an English interface can export an Arabic, right-to-left chart.
📄

Arabic in exported documentsWhat to expect, and what to check

PDF. Arabic letters are shaped and joined correctly, the text is reordered for right-to-left reading, and the Arabic font is embedded inside the file — so the document looks the same on a colleague’s machine, a printer, or a phone, whether or not that device has the font installed.

Word. Arabic runs are marked as Arabic text with right-to-left direction, tables are mirrored, and the document font covers the Arabic range — so Word applies its own Arabic typography instead of treating the text as unknown Latin.

File names. Arabic project names survive into the downloaded file name instead of being stripped away.

🌐
This guide has its own switcher at the top of the page, and it opens in whatever language you were using in the tool. You can also link to a language directly by adding ?lang=ar, ?lang=fr or ?lang=en to the address.

⚙️

Export appearance settingsNew

1

Open ⚙️ Settings from the sidebar — it sits just above Help — and stay on its Export tab.

2

Pick a heading colour and a table header colour from nine presets. Both apply to the PDF and the Word files — and the header colour also to the Skills Level Matrix on screen.

3

Adjust the font size if you want the document larger or smaller — a relative shift from −2 to +4 pt, which moves every size together and keeps the existing heading hierarchy. This one applies to Word only.

4

Press 💾 Save. Nothing reaches a document until you do — Cancel discards whatever you were trying out.

ℹ️
Text sitting on a shaded cell or bar is set to white or black automatically, by measured contrast — so a dark table header never leaves you with dark text on a dark fill.
🎨
The settings are shared across all three languages and stay until you change them. ↺ Reset to defaults restores the original look, and like every other control here it only takes effect once you press Save. The defaults reproduce the previous appearance exactly, so nothing changes until you choose to change it.
💡
The JSON file name uses the sidebar project name — not the occupation title — so a project you renamed after importing still exports under the name you see on screen.
🛡
If the verification data is still an unverified generated draft, it is left out of the exported documents and the report says so. Accept it in the Task Verification tab first if you want it included.
📥 Import

Importing projects

Bring exported JSON files back in — one at a time, or a whole folder at once.

Single and multi-file import

1

Click ⬆ Import in the toolbar and select one or several .json files.

2

Each file becomes a separate project in the sidebar. The last one imported becomes active.

3

The project name comes from the file name, with the date and time suffix stripped — so Welding_2026-03-14_22-45.json becomes a project called Welding.

📴 Offline

Offline use and updatesNew

Training rooms lose connectivity. The tool is built to keep working when that happens.

📲
Install itUse your browser’s Install or Add to home screen option. It then opens like any other app, from its own icon.
✅
What works offlineEvery tab and every export — the PDF and Word libraries ship with the tool — the Arabic font, individual rating, and this guide if you opened it once while online.
🚫
What needs the networkAI generation of any kind, and live workshop voting. Both fail cleanly and tell you why.
🔔
Update bannerWhen a new version is published, a small banner offers to update. Nothing changes until you accept.
⏳
Loading screenWhile the tool starts, a loading screen shows the version and date. On a slow connection it says so, and offers Reload or Continue.
📶
Before a workshop with poor connectivity: open the tool online once so everything is cached, generate whatever drafts you need in advance, and export a JSON backup. You can then run the whole session offline and collect ratings individually.
💡 Tips

Tips and best practices

📋
Chart Info first, alwaysOccupation title and scope of work set the boundary for every generated stage. Ten minutes here saves an hour of correction later.
✨
Draft before the workshop, not instead of itBring a generated draft into the room as a provocation for the panel to argue with. The authority of the chart comes from the practitioners.
📸
Snapshot before generatingAny generation that replaces content deserves a named checkpoint first. It is faster than undo and survives a reload.
💾
Export after every phaseExport the JSON after duties, after verification, after clustering. The file name carries date and time, so the files version themselves.
🃏
Card view in the roomIt mirrors the sticky-card wall the panel already knows, which keeps the discussion on the content rather than on the software.
📊
Table view for bulk workSwitch to it when pasting from a spreadsheet or restructuring many tasks at once.
🔢
Pick the formula deliberatelyI×F for standard priority. I×F×D when you need to estimate training load and sequence the curriculum.
📡
Rehearse the live sessionRun a two-minute test with a colleague on the venue’s network before the panel arrives.
📁
One project per occupationName projects meaningfully — the name becomes the export file name and the label you search by.
↩
Undo covers structureAdding and removing duties and tasks is undoable. Fast typing inside a card collapses into a single undo step.
🔬
Analyse the priority tasks firstStart Task Analysis with the tasks at the top of the priority ranking — they carry most of the training time.
📜
Check the standard before the outcomesThe Occupational Standard’s missing list shows ranges and criteria that would leave outcomes without a base.
🏷
Settle module codes before the curriculumCodes and short names travel into the curriculum files; fix them in Module Mapping before exporting.
